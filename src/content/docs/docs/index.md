<section class="docs-section" aria-labelledby="qol-title">
  <p class="docs-label">Around here QOL means</p>
  <h2 id="qol-title">Quality of life with opinions</h2>
  <ul class="feature-list">
    <li>Fix what is obviously wrong or ill-designed. Keep the fix small.</li>
    <li>Start with what Omarchy already ships. Reinventing is work, not progress.</li>
    <li>Stay quiet when idle (a tray widget does not need a data center).</li>
    <li>
      Ship an uninstall button (so sad), so you can remove my <strong>BLOAT</strong> from your beloved system :) I&apos;d
      rather get an issue first, tho, but the world got so busy with AI...
    </li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section class="docs-section" aria-labelledby="vision-title">
  <p class="docs-label">Why bother</p>
  <h2 id="vision-title">Because minimalism and taste apply to your plugins as well.</h2>
  <div class="vision-grid docs-vision-grid">
    <p class="vision-lead">
      A plugin should solve the thing that annoyed you, then get out of the way. There is no reason to bloat your system. Same things applied back then, when neovim was a thing and people started to "install every available plugin".
    </p>
    <div class="vision-copy">
      <p>
        These plugins started as small fixes for my own Omarchy setup, already in one or another form on the Omarchy 3.8.X versions. When something felt obviously wrong, I change the smallest useful piece instead of rebuilding half the desktop.
      </p>
      <p>Native controls, low idle cost, and an uninstall that cleans up after itself. Nothing revolutionary.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
